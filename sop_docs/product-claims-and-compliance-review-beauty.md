# Product Claims and Compliance Review (Beauty)

## Purpose

Owner: Beauty Compliance team. Category: Compliance.

This procedure sets out how Beauty reviews product claims and presentation before a livestream. Customers rely on what hosts say and show when deciding whether a product is right for them, so claims must be accurate, supportable, and consistent with the product information the brand has approved. A claim that sounds stronger than the available evidence can mislead customers and create compliance risk for the company and the brand. Review also covers visual material and the way product details appear during the stream, because photos and on-screen information can communicate claims just as directly as spoken words. The review gives the team time to identify unsupported wording, request evidence or consent, and correct the script before it is used. Hosts and producers should treat approval as part of stream preparation, not as a final check after the show is ready.

## How It Works

The script must be reviewed 3 business days before the stream. Send the complete script and any planned product visuals to the Beauty Compliance team, including claims, demonstrations, before-and-after photos, and on-screen product information. Review the full presentation rather than only isolated phrases: a combination of spoken wording, visuals, and product details can imply a claim that is not stated outright. If the team identifies an issue, update the script or materials and return them for review before the stream. Do not assume that a claim is acceptable because it appears in brand copy or has been used in another stream; the current product and supporting materials must be checked.

Core steps:

1. Submit the script and planned visuals for review 3 business days before the stream.
2. Resolve review comments and confirm the final version with the Beauty Compliance team.
3. Use only the approved script and materials during the stream.

Scripts must not say “cures”, “medical grade”, “guaranteed anti-aging” or “100% natural” unless the product is certified. These terms can imply medical effects, a guarantee, or a specific standard, so do not include them based on informal assurances or general product descriptions. If the product is certified, provide the relevant support to the reviewer and use the wording only as approved. An SPF claim needs the product’s test report; do not make or display the claim until that report has been provided and reviewed. Evidence should support the product being sold, not merely a similar item or a general ingredient claim.

Before-and-after photos need the brand’s written consent and must not be retouched. Consent helps confirm that the brand has approved the images for use, while unretouched photos reduce the risk of presenting an altered result as typical or genuine. When a skincare product is introduced, its ingredient list stays on screen for 10 seconds. Keep the list readable and visible for the full display period so customers have a fair chance to review what the product contains.

## Common Mistakes

A common mistake is treating a phrase as acceptable because it is familiar in beauty sales. The wording still needs certification or evidence where required. Another is using an SPF claim before the test report has been reviewed, or using before-and-after photos without written consent. Do not retouch photos to improve how a result looks. Do not remove the ingredient list too quickly or cover it with other graphics during the required display.

## Who to Ask

Contact the Beauty Compliance team with questions about claims, evidence, consent, or on-screen presentation. If a change is needed close to the stream, ask them before using the revised wording or materials.

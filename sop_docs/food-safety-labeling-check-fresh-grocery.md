# Food Safety Labeling Check (Fresh Grocery)

## Purpose

Owner: Fresh Grocery Quality team. Category: Compliance.

This procedure helps Fresh Grocery staff check that product information shown during a livestream is clear and supported before an item is offered for sale. Customers use these details to decide whether a food is suitable for them and when it can be used. Missing allergen information can expose customers to ingredients they need to avoid, while an unsupported origin or organic claim can mislead them about what they are buying. Date checks also help prevent food from shipping too close to its best-before date. The check applies to product information on screen and to the product prepared for shipment. Staff should resolve gaps before the product is introduced or released for shipping, rather than relying on a correction after a customer has placed an order.

## How It Works

Check the product and its supporting information before the livestream introduction. Confirm that the on-screen details match the product being sold, including allergen information, origin details where required, and any organic claim. For shipment, check the best-before date on the food itself. These checks cover separate risks: a correct on-screen description does not confirm the date on the stock that will ship, and a suitable date does not make an incomplete product description acceptable. If a required detail is missing, unclear, or unsupported, pause the introduction or shipment until the Fresh Grocery Quality team has advised what to do.

1. Confirm allergens are shown on screen when the product is introduced.
2. Check the best-before date on the product selected for shipment.
3. Confirm country of origin for meat and seafood, and verify the certificate number for any organic claim.
4. Hold the item if a check fails, and ask the Fresh Grocery Quality team for guidance.

Allergens such as nuts and shellfish must be shown on screen when the product is introduced. Customers may need this information before deciding whether to buy, so the relevant allergen details should be visible at the point the product is presented, not left for customers to infer from the product name or a later discussion. Check that the on-screen information corresponds to the item being introduced. If the details are missing or do not match the product, do not proceed with the introduction until the information has been checked and corrected.

For chilled food, the product must have at least 10 days left before its best-before date when it ships. For dry goods, it must have at least 90 days left before its best-before date when it ships. Check the date on the actual product selected for the order, since stock may have different dates even when it is the same item. If the product does not meet the applicable requirement, do not ship it. Ask the Fresh Grocery Quality team to confirm the next step.

The country of origin must be stated for meat and seafood. Any organic claim must include the certificate number. Confirm that these details are available and apply to the product being sold; do not add an origin or organic statement based on assumption alone.

## Common Mistakes

A common mistake is treating an allergen mention in conversation as a substitute for showing the information on screen when the product is introduced. Another is checking a sample product’s date instead of the unit prepared for shipment. Staff can also overlook the different remaining-date requirements for chilled food and dry goods, omit the country of origin for meat or seafood, or use an organic claim without its certificate number. If information is absent or does not match the product, do not guess or leave the issue for the customer to resolve.

## Who to Ask

Contact the Fresh Grocery Quality team when a label, date, origin statement, or organic certificate detail is unclear or does not meet this procedure. Hold the introduction or shipment until the team confirms the next step.

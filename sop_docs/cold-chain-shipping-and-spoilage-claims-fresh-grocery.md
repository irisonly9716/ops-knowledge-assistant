# Cold Chain Shipping and Spoilage Claims (Fresh Grocery)

## Purpose

Owner: Fresh Grocery Logistics team. Category: Fulfillment.

Fresh grocery can spoil if it is exposed to warm conditions or delayed in transit. This procedure sets clear shipping and claim rules so customers receive safe products and the team can respond consistently when a shipment arrives spoiled. Frozen items ship with dry ice and spend at most 48 hours in transit. They do not ship on Fridays, which prevents them from sitting in a depot over the weekend. These limits reduce the time frozen products are exposed to conditions that could compromise them. When spoilage occurs despite these controls, a timely claim with a photo gives the team useful evidence to assess the issue and address the customer’s needs. The customer chooses between a refund and a new shipment. The team also reviews carrier performance when claims exceed 3% of a shipment, so recurring problems can be identified and addressed. Follow the steps below for every relevant shipment and spoilage claim.

## How It Works

1. Check that frozen items are packed with dry ice and scheduled to spend at most 48 hours in transit.
2. Do not ship frozen items on Fridays; this avoids weekend depot delays.
3. If a customer reports spoilage, request a claim within 24 hours of delivery and ask for a photo.
4. Offer the customer a choice between a refund and a new shipment.
5. If claims exceed 3% of a shipment, review the carrier.

Keep the claim process straightforward. Use the delivery time to determine whether the claim was made within 24 hours, and make sure the photo is attached to the claim record. The photo helps the team understand the reported condition instead of relying only on a description. Record the customer’s chosen resolution and route the case through the normal fulfillment process.

The shipping rules are preventive controls, not a guarantee that spoilage cannot occur. Dry ice supports frozen products during transit, while the transit limit and Friday restriction reduce exposure to delays. If a shipment misses its expected timing or arrives with a problem, handle the customer’s report under the claim process rather than assuming the packaging controls were sufficient.

Review the carrier when claims exceed 3% of a shipment. Look at the shipment’s claim records and available delivery information to understand whether carrier handling or delays may have contributed. Escalate the review through the Fresh Grocery Logistics team so the carrier issue can be considered and followed up.

## Common Mistakes

Do not schedule frozen items to ship on Fridays. A shipment can then remain at a depot over the weekend, increasing the risk of spoilage.

Do not omit dry ice or allow frozen items to spend more than 48 hours in transit.

Do not treat a spoilage report as a complete claim without a photo, or overlook the requirement that the claim be made within 24 hours of delivery.

Do not choose the resolution for the customer. The customer decides between a refund and a new shipment.

Do not ignore the carrier review threshold. When claims exceed 3% of a shipment, start a carrier review.

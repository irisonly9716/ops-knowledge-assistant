# Campaign documents (plans / reviews)

The campaign plans and reviews of the evaluation corpus: `documents` rows with `doc_type = campaign_plan` /
`campaign_review`, in Markdown. They go through the same ingestion pipeline as [sop_docs/](../sop_docs/); they
live in a separate folder only because they mean something different.

## Format

```markdown
---
doc_type: campaign_plan or campaign_review
title: Document title
department: Department name (must match departments.name exactly)
campaign_name: Name of the campaign
campaign_start: The campaign's start date, which tells campaigns of the same name apart
host: Lead host
---

Body...
```

The documents describe one campaign, but `documents.campaign_id` is a NOT NULL foreign key
whose value only exists in the database. The upload picks the campaign explicitly
(`campaignId`); the other fields here are for people reading the file, and for
`scripts/ingest_corpus.py`, which finds the campaign by department, name and start date.

**The numbers are real mock data.** Every table and figure comes from what
`llm-service/scripts/generate_mock_data.py` generates: GMV is the sum of the daily metrics, the conversion
rate their average, peak viewers their maximum, and ROI the total product profit over the actual operating
cost. The generator is seeded and dates are computed from a fixed `AS_OF` date, so every run produces the
same rows and these documents stay correct, as long as the generator and its pinned faker version don't
change. If you change it, generate the documents again.

Two documents, for `Beauty Black Friday Live` (`campaign_id = 1`), were written by hand. The other 53 were
generated by `llm-service/scripts/generate_corpus.py`: the script renders the tables from the database, a
model writes the prose around them, and a draft that quotes a number not in its facts is rejected and
written again. The targets of a plan aren't in the database: they are seeded around what happened, so some
campaigns meet them and some don't, and the plan and its review always agree. The plans name no prices,
because the mock data's average prices agree with SQL but aren't believable (instant ramen at $207).

## The two document types

- **campaign_plan**: written before the campaign goes live: product lineup, pricing and
  targets. AI later compares it with the results to explain which choices led to which
  outcome, and it is a reference for planning similar campaigns.
- **campaign_review**: written after the campaign: **ROI and per-product results live in
  this document**, not in structured fields. That is the project's principle in practice:
  AI analyzes content and results, it does not recompute metrics.

## What makes retrieval hard on purpose

- **Campaigns of the same name**, told apart only by their dates: three Home New Product Launch campaigns
  that overlap in January 2026, two Beauty Fan Appreciation Day campaigns that both start in February 2026.
  Their titles carry the start date, and so does their citation name in the database (`document_catalog`).
- **A missing document**: three campaigns have no plan, two have no review, one of them because it is still
  running. A question about them should get a refusal, not another campaign's answer.
- **Different authors**: plans are written in three ways (standard, brief, memo), reviews in four (standard,
  recap, story, wins), and a campaign's plan and review often have different authors.

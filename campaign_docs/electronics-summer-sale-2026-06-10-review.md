# Electronics Summer Sale - Post-Campaign Review

## How It Went

The Consumer Electronics team ran the Electronics Summer Sale from Jun 10, 2026 to Jul 14, 2026, with Julie King hosting streams on 35 days. The campaign delivered a mixed result: conversion rate finished above target, peak concurrent viewers landed within the target range, and GMV came in below the target range. That combination points to a stream that converted viewers effectively but did not generate enough total sales to meet the GMV goal. The operating cost also finished above plan, so the campaign had less room to absorb the shortfall. Looking at the results together, the next review should focus on how traffic volume, product mix, and offer performance contributed to sales, rather than treating conversion alone as proof that the campaign reached its full potential. The product results show that the Smartwatch contributed the most sales and profit, while the Power Bank had the highest return rate. Those differences are useful starting points for planning future product lineups and setting expectations for returns.

The campaign held audience attention and converted well, but missed its GMV target.

## Results

GMV was below target, while conversion rate exceeded its target range. Peak concurrent viewers were on target. Operating cost was over budget, and total product profit was higher than actual operating cost.

| Metric | Target (see the campaign plan) | Actual | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| GMV | $1,730K-$2,360K | $1.72M | Below target |
| Conversion rate | 6%-8% | 8.01% | Above target |
| Peak concurrent viewers | 15,000-17,000 | 15,568 | On target |
| Operating cost | Planned $182.7K | Actual $186.7K | 2.2% over budget |

- Total product profit over the 4 featured products, product cost already deducted: **$606.9K**
- Actual operating cost: **$186.7K**
- **ROI = total product profit / operating cost = 606.9 / 186.7 ≈ 3.25**

## Products

The Smartwatch was the largest contributor, with 2,498 units sold, $630.3K in sales, and $370.2K in profit. Its 58.73% margin was the highest among the listed products. Wireless Earbuds also delivered strong profit, at $135.5K, with a 55.46% margin. Its sales were $244.4K from 2,391 units.

Charging Cable sold 1,156 units and generated $255.1K in sales, but its 32.19% margin was lower than the other listed products. Power Bank contributed $33.5K in sales and $19.0K in profit from 559 units. It also had the highest return rate, at 15.9%, so its returns deserve review before it is given a larger role in a future campaign.

| Product | Units sold | Sales | Profit | Margin | Return rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smartwatch | 2,498 | $630.3K | $370.2K | 58.73% | 13.0% |
| Charging Cable | 1,156 | $255.1K | $82.1K | 32.19% | 9.7% |
| Wireless Earbuds | 2,391 | $244.4K | $135.5K | 55.46% | 10.8% |
| Power Bank | 559 | $33.5K | $19.0K | 56.82% | 15.9% |

## Lessons

Keep the Smartwatch central to the product plan, while checking whether its sales contribution can help close the gap to the GMV target. Review how Wireless Earbuds can support the lineup alongside the leading product.

Investigate Power Bank returns and Charging Cable margin before repeating the same mix. The campaign’s above-target conversion rate did not prevent GMV from falling short, so planning should account for the sales contribution of each product, not just conversion.

Keep operating costs closer to plan. The result supports the campaign’s profitability, but spending still exceeded budget.
